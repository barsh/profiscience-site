Introduction
### The High Cost of Getting CLE Wrong
&#9662;
A five-minute read that could save your firm hundreds of hours
Every firm has a CLE story that starts with good intentions and ends with unnecessary chaos—lost hours, lost revenue, and anxious partners. The truth? CLE (or CPD, as it’s known in many regions) isn’t red tape. It’s strategic infrastructure.
When managed well, CLE protects revenue, reputation, and morale—freeing lawyers to focus on client work instead of compliance firefighting. When managed poorly, it quietly drains thousands of billable hours every year.
Whether you’re a Director of Professional Development, HR leader, or CLE specialist, this resource helps you start the conversation about turning compliance from a burden into a strategic advantage.

### The Hidden Risks
&#9662;

# Should your content provider also determine how your firm manages learning?
Foley & Lardner wanted to expand development opportunities without sending learners to another destination or making one content provider the center of its learning program. THE CHALLENGE
### More content does not mean more learning.
Foley wanted to expand access to power-skills, technical, and professional-development content for several employee groups.
LinkedIn Learning offered a broad library, but access alone did not determine which content was most relevant to each role, how employees should progress through it, or how the firm would assign, track, and report on participation.
THE PLATFORM DECISION
### Keep content choice separate from learning management.
A content provider’s platform is naturally designed to deliver its own content. Most law firms, however, build learning programs from many sources.
Foley also needed to manage internal programs, firm-created resources, assignments, reminders, progress, and reporting through a consistent learning environment.
UniversitySite allowed LinkedIn Learning to become one source within the firm's broader learning operation rather than a separate destination or the system defining how learning would be managed.
THE APPROACH
### Curate the content. Build a clear path through it.
Foley used the LinkedIn Learning Extension to make selected content available through UniversitySite.
Administrators could select full courses or individual course chapters, allowing the firm to focus each plan on the material most relevant to a specific audience.
The firm then organized that content into tailored learning plans for legal assistants, technology staff, and managers. External content could be combined with the firm's existing learning resources and managed through the same administrative environment.
THE LEARNER EXPERIENCE
### One learning experience. Multiple content sources.
Employees accessed LinkedIn Learning content through UniversitySite alongside firm learning and assigned programs.
Single sign-on reduced the need to navigate a separate login, while structured learning plans made the next step clear.
Rather than asking employees to search a large catalog and decide what mattered, Foley could present content selected for their role and development needs.
THE ADMINISTRATIVE EXPERIENCE
### One place to assign, remind, track, and report.
- Assign role-specific learning plans
- Include full courses or selected chapters
- Send reminders
- Track progress and completion
- Report across employee groups
- Manage external and internal learning through one environment
The integration created value not simply by exposing LinkedIn Learning content, but by placing that content inside the firm's broader learning operation.

### A structured development program with 90% participation.

### The content provider can change. The learning operation does not have to.
Law-firm learning programs draw from many sources: internal faculty, live programs, firm-created resources, required learning, and external libraries.
When the learning platform is tied too closely to one provider’s catalog, content selection can begin to determine how the entire program is administered.
UniversitySite serves a different role. It allows firms to organize learning from multiple sources through consistent plans, assignments, reminders, reporting, and learner experiences.
For Foley, LinkedIn Learning became part of the firm’s broader development strategy rather than a separate destination.
More broadly, a content-agnostic platform allows a firm to choose content based on relevance and quality while preserving the learning structure surrounding it.
The content provider can change. The firm's learning operation does not have to.
